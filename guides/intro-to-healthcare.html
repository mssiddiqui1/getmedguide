Introduction to Healthcare

A big-picture map of the healthcare industry — sectors, players, coverage types, and where coding and billing careers fit in.

What "the healthcare industry" actually includes

Healthcare isn't one industry — it's several connected ones, each with its own workforce, rules, and revenue model.

SectorExamples
Ambulatory / outpatientPhysician offices, urgent care, ambulatory surgery centers
Inpatient / facilityHospitals, inpatient rehab, skilled nursing facilities
PayersMedicare, Medicaid, commercial insurers, Medicare Advantage plans
Pharma & devicesDrug manufacturers, medical device companies, pharmacies
Health IT & servicesEHR vendors, billing companies, coding/auditing firms, analytics

The continuum of care

Most patients don't stay in one part of the system — they move along a continuum depending on what they need at the time.

The continuum of care: most patients move along this line, not through just one stop on it.

A single patient might touch every stage in one year: an annual wellness visit (prevention), a primary care follow-up, a specialist referral, a same-day surgery (acute), a few weeks of physical therapy (post-acute), and eventually long-term support. Every one of those stops generates its own documentation, coding, and billing.

Who's actually involved

  • Providers — physicians, nurses, and other clinicians delivering care
  • Patients — the people receiving it, and increasingly, paying a share of it directly
  • Payers — the entities that reimburse for care (government programs and private insurers)
  • Regulators — CMS (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) and HHS at the federal level, plus state health departments and licensing boards

Public vs. private coverage, at a glance

Coverage typeWho it's for
MedicareAge 65+, and some people with qualifying disabilities

Where coding and billing careers fit

Every stop along the continuum of care needs someone to translate what happened clinically into the codes and claims that get the provider paid accurately. That's the coding and billing workforce — sitting between the clinical side and the payment side, at every sector in the table above.

Note: if you're deciding which certification path fits you, the sector you want to work in matters as much as the credential itself — outpatient-focused roles, inpatient-facility roles, and payer-side risk adjustment roles each map to different exams.
