Introduction to CCA

What the Certified Coding Associate credential covers, who it's for, and how it compares to CPC.

What CCA is

The Certified Coding Associate (CCA) is an entry-level coding credential from AHIMA (the American Health Information Management Association). It's designed to prove foundational coding competency across both inpatient and outpatient settings — before you've necessarily specialized in one.

Who it's for

CCA is aimed at people just starting a coding career, including recent graduates of coding programs, career-changers, and anyone who wants a credential that doesn't assume years of on-the-job experience yet. AHIMA recommends (but doesn't strictly require) some coursework or experience before sitting for it.

CCA is AHIMA's entry point — many coders use it as a stepping stone toward CCS or CCS-P.

Exam format

DetailWhat to expect
Administered byAHIMA
FormatMultiple choice, computer-based
SettingBoth inpatient and outpatient coding scenarios
Code sets testedICD-10-CM, ICD-10-PCS (basics), CPT, HCPCS Level II

What's tested

  • Coding knowledge across diagnosis and procedure code sets
  • Health record content and documentation basics
  • Regulatory compliance and reimbursement methodology fundamentals
  • Data quality and coding guidelines

CCA vs. CPC: what's the actual difference?

CCA (AHIMA)CPC (AAPC)
Setting focusBoth inpatient and outpatient basicsPhysician / outpatient professional coding
Best forCoders wanting a broad, entry-level foundationCoders heading straight into a physician-practice role
Common next stepCCS or CCS-P (AHIMA)Specialty CPC credentials (AAPC)

Neither is objectively "better" — the right one depends on the type of employer and setting you're targeting. Many working coders eventually hold credentials from both organizations.

Study tips

  • Get comfortable navigating the ICD-10-CM and CPT code books (or their digital equivalents) — speed matters as much as knowledge
  • Practice with real, redacted health record excerpts, not just definitions
  • Review AHIMA's official exam content outline before you schedule — it tells you exactly what's weighted heavily
