Introduction to Nursing

The nursing credential ladder, the nursing process, and why nursing documentation matters so much to coders and billers.

What nurses actually do

Nursing is the clinical backbone of most care settings — nurses assess patients, carry out the plan of care, administer treatments and medications, monitor for changes, educate patients and families, and coordinate with the rest of the care team. Their documentation also happens to be some of the richest source material a coder will ever read.

The Nursing Process (ADPIE)

Nurses are trained to work through patient care using a structured, repeatable framework:

The Nursing Process (ADPIE) repeats for every patient problem — Evaluation often loops back into a new Assessment.

  • Assessment — collect data: vitals, symptoms, history, observations
  • Diagnosis — identify the patient's problem (a nursing diagnosis, distinct from a physician's medical diagnosis)
  • Planning — set measurable goals and choose interventions
  • Implementation — carry out the plan: treatments, education, medications
  • Evaluation — check whether the goal was met, and adjust

Where nurses work

Hospitals (inpatient units, ED, ICU), physician offices, outpatient surgery centers, skilled nursing and long-term care facilities, home health, hospice, schools, and telehealth — nursing roles exist in nearly every setting on the continuum of care.

Why nursing documentation matters to coders and billers

Nurses often document details that directly support code assignment, even though they aren't the ones assigning the code:

  • Present on Admission (POA) indicators often rely on nursing assessment notes from admission
  • Wound staging and skin assessments support pressure ulcer and wound care coding
  • Fall risk, nutrition, and pain assessments can support medical necessity for services billed
  • Time-based interventions (e.g., critical care time, infusion start/stop times) frequently come from the nursing flowsheet, not the physician note

Note: scope of practice for LPNs, RNs, and APRNs varies by state — what one state allows an APRN to do independently, another may require physician oversight for. This matters when you're coding services and checking who's licensed to perform or bill for them.

If you're coming from a nursing background into coding

Clinical experience is a genuine advantage for a future coder or auditor — you already understand disease processes, treatment sequences, and clinical documentation norms. What's new is translating that knowledge into the specific rules of ICD-10-CM, CPT, and payer policy, which the certification-specific guides in this library walk through.
