Revenue Cycle Pocket Guide

A condensed reference for every stage of the revenue cycle — registration through collections — with the terms you're expected to know.

What is the revenue cycle?

The full financial lifecycle of a patient account — from scheduling to final payment. The goal: get providers accurately and fully paid for care, quickly and compliantly. It splits into three phases: front-end (before the visit), mid-cycle (coding & charges), and back-end (claims & collections).

Front, mid, and back office

PhaseCovers
Front-endScheduling, registration, eligibility, prior authorization, patient estimates
Mid-cycleCharge capture, coding, clinical documentation improvement (CDI), charge reconciliation
Back-endClaims, payment posting, denials & appeals, A/R follow-up, collections

The 13-step RCM lifecycle

#Step
1Pre-registration & scheduling
2Registration / patient intake
3Insurance eligibility & verification
4Prior authorization / referral
5Encounter & documentation
6Charge capture
7Medical coding (ICD-10-CM, CPT, HCPCS)
8Claim scrubbing & submission

10Payment / remittance posting
11Denial management & appeals
12Patient statements & collections
13Reporting & analytics

Charge capture means recording every billable service and supply from the encounter. Coding means translating that documentation into ICD-10-CM (diagnoses) and CPT/HCPCS (services). Charge reconciliation confirms all services were actually captured and charged — accuracy here drives clean claims and fewer denials downstream.

Rejections vs. denials

TermWhat it means
RejectionThe claim never entered the payer's system (a front-end/format error) — fix it and resubmit
DenialThe claim was processed but not paid — research it, then correct or appeal
CARC / RARCCodes on the remittance advice that explain why a claim was adjusted or denied

Common denial reasons

  • Eligibility or coverage inactive at the time of service
  • Missing or invalid prior authorization
  • Lack of medical necessity — the diagnosis doesn't support the service billed
  • Coding errors, bundling issues, or NCCI edit conflicts
  • Missing or incorrect information on the claim
  • Duplicate claim submission
  • Timely filing deadline missed

Appeals & timely filing

  • Timely filing is the window to submit a claim — it varies by payer (Medicare, for example, allows one year from the date of service).
  • An appeal formally challenges a denial with corrected information or supporting documentation.
  • Appeal levels typically escalate: redetermination → reconsideration → a higher-level review.
  • Track deadlines closely — a missed filing window usually means the claim is lost for good.

Key RCM metrics (KPIs)

MetricWhat it measures
Days in A/RAverage days to collect (target: roughly 40–50 or fewer)
Clean claim rate% of claims accepted the first time (target: 95%+)
First-pass rate% of claims paid on the first submission
Denial rate% of claims denied (target: under roughly 5–10%)
Net collection rateAmount collected vs. amount collectible, after adjustments
Cost to collectExpense incurred per dollar collected
A/R > 90 daysAged receivables — lower is better

A/R aging is typically tracked in buckets: 0–30 → 31–60 → 61–90 → 90+ days. The older a balance gets, the harder it is to collect.

RCM best practices

  • Verify eligibility and authorization up front — this stops most denials before they start.
  • Collect copays and estimates at the point of service.
  • Code accurately and scrub claims before submission.
  • Work denials fast, before the timely-filing window runs out.
  • Monitor KPIs and A/R aging weekly.
  • Keep documentation clear and complete — it supports every step downstream.
  • Work the oldest and highest-dollar accounts first. DNFB (Discharged, Not Final Billed) is a key backlog metric to watch.
