CPC Exam Pocket Guide

A condensed, exam-day reference covering the high-yield rules and reminders that show up again and again on the CPC exam.

Exam at a glance

  • 100 multiple-choice questions (including ~10 case studies)
  • 4 hours, one session → roughly 2 min 24 sec per question
  • Open book — your printed CPT, ICD-10-CM & HCPCS manuals
  • Pass mark: 70% · results in roughly 7–10 business days
  • Taken online (live remote proctor) or in person
  • Voucher often includes one free retake
  • Open book ≠ easy — speed of lookup is the real skill
Exam content areas: CPT — Surgery (by body system), E/M, Anesthesia, Radiology, Path/Lab, Medicine; ICD-10-CM diagnosis coding; HCPCS Level II; medical terminology & anatomy; coding guidelines & modifiers; compliance & regulatory (HIPAA, NCCI, fraud/abuse); practice management. CPT and E/M together make up roughly half the exam.

Key CPT guidelines

  • Global surgical package: pre-op, intra-op & routine post-op are bundled (0/10/90-day periods)
  • "Separate procedure": bundled with a related larger procedure; billable when done alone
  • Add-on (+) codes: report with a primary code, never alone; modifier-51 exempt
  • Unlisted code: use when no specific code exists; attach documentation
  • Don't unbundle — watch NCCI edits

Anesthesia & radiology

Anesthesia: (Base + Time + Modifying Units) × Conversion Factor. Time unit ≈ 15 min. Physical status modifiers P1–P6 describe the patient's health for anesthesia.

Radiology components: 26 = professional (read) · TC = technical (equipment) · no modifier = global.

ICD-10-CM guideline reminders

  • Always: Alphabetic Index → verify in Tabular List (never code from the Index alone)
  • 7th character: A = initial · D = subsequent · S = sequela; use "X" as a placeholder for empty positions
  • "Code first / use additional code" = etiology before manifestation
  • Combination codes capture two conditions in one
  • Code to the highest specificity & use laterality
  • Unspecified only when documentation won't support more
  • Signs/symptoms not coded if a definitive diagnosis is documented
Chapter order (21): Infectious → Neoplasms → Blood/immune → Endocrine → Mental → Nervous → Eye → Ear → Circulatory → Respiratory → Digestive → Skin → Musculoskeletal → Genitourinary → Pregnancy → Perinatal → Congenital → Symptoms (R) → Injury (S/T) → External causes (V–Y) → Factors/Z codes. Z codes = reasons for a visit other than illness (screening, status).

E/M quick guide (2021+)

  • Office visits leveled by Medical Decision-Making (MDM) or total time
  • MDM = number of problems + data reviewed + risk
  • New patient 99202–99205 · Established 99211–99215
  • New = not seen by the practice/specialty in 3 years
  • History & exam must be medically appropriate but no longer set the level

Open-book & exam-day strategy

  • Tab your manuals by section — highlighting OK, long handwritten notes are not
  • 3-pass method: easy first → flagged lookups → case studies last
  • Never spend more than ~4 min on one question — flag & move on
  • Eliminate two wrong answers, then verify the code in the book
  • Read E/M & op-note cases twice — the detail picks the code
  • Watch qualifiers: "except," "not," "most/least"
  • No blanks — no penalty for guessing

Smart study plan

  • Weeks 1–2: terminology + anatomy; start tabbing books
  • Weeks 3–4: ICD-10-CM guidelines & drills
  • Weeks 5–7: CPT by section (heavy on Surgery & E/M)
  • Week 8: HCPCS + modifiers
  • Weeks 9–10: full timed practice exams; review every miss
  • Official AAPC practice exams mirror the real test best

Study aid only — a quick reference, not a substitute for official AAPC materials or current-year code manuals. Code sets, ranges, and exam rules update annually; always confirm against your current CPT, ICD-10-CM, and HCPCS books and aapc.com.
