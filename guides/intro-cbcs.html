Introduction to CBCS

What the NHA's Certified Billing and Coding Specialist exam covers, and who the combined billing-and-coding role fits.

What CBCS is

The Certified Billing and Coding Specialist (CBCS) credential is administered by the NHA (National Healthcareer Association). Unlike credentials that focus tightly on coding alone, CBCS is built around the combined billing-and-coding role that many smaller physician practices actually hire for — one person handling both.

Who it's for

CBCS fits people entering a front-office or back-office role at a smaller practice, urgent care, or billing service where coding and billing aren't separate departments. It's also a common credential for graduates of shorter career-training programs.

How coding and billing connect

CBCS covers this whole chain — not just the coding step, but everything from charge entry through payment posting.

Coding assigns the right codes to a service; billing takes those codes and turns them into a paid claim. CBCS tests both halves of that chain, not just the first step.

Exam domains

DomainCovers
Regulatory complianceHIPAA, fraud and abuse basics, documentation requirements
Billing and reimbursementClaim forms, payment methodologies, patient statements
CodingICD-10-CM, CPT, HCPCS Level II fundamentals
Insurance and claimsPayer types, eligibility, prior authorization, denials
Front office / administrationScheduling, registration, and the patient-facing side of billing

Eligibility

NHA typically expects candidates to have completed a relevant training program or have equivalent work experience — check NHA's current candidate handbook for the specifics, since eligibility rules can change.

CBCS vs. CCA/CPC: broader, not narrower

CCA and CPC focus specifically on code assignment. CBCS deliberately tests the billing side alongside coding, which makes it a strong fit if the job you want is titled "billing and coding specialist" rather than "coder."

Study tips

  • Learn the CMS-1500 and UB-04 claim forms — not just what codes go where, but what each field means
  • Understand the claim lifecycle end to end: submission, adjudication, EOB, denial, appeal
  • Don't neglect the compliance and front-office domains — they carry real weight on this exam
