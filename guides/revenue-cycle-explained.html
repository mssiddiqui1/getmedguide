The Revenue Cycle, Explained

How a patient visit turns into payment — the six stages, step by step.

What is the revenue cycle?

The revenue cycle is the full financial journey of a patient account — from scheduling all the way to final payment. It runs across three phases:

  • Front-end — registration, eligibility & authorization
  • Mid-cycle — charge capture & coding
  • Back-end — claims, payment, denials & billing

The goal across all three phases: get providers accurately and fully paid for the care they deliver — quickly and compliantly.

The six stages at a glance

#StagePhase
1Registration & eligibilityFront-end
2Prior authorizationFront-end
3Charge capture & codingMid-cycle
4Claim submissionBack-end
5Adjudication & paymentBack-end
6Denials, appeals & billingBack-end

1. Registration & eligibility

The patient is registered and their identity confirmed, and their insurance coverage is verified — is it active, and what does it actually cover? Most denials trace back to this stage, so front-end accuracy is critical to everything downstream.

2. Prior authorization

Many services need the payer's approval before they're performed, and referrals are obtained where the health plan requires them. Missing authorization is one of the single most common reasons claims get denied later.

3. Charge capture & coding

Every billable service and supply from the visit is recorded, and coders translate the provider's documentation into ICD-10-CM, CPT, and HCPCS codes. Accurate coding here means clean claims and fewer denials downstream — this is where coders sit at the center of the whole cycle.

4. Claim submission

The claim is "scrubbed" for errors, then sent to the payer — usually electronically (an 837 transaction) through a clearinghouse. A clean claim is one that processes without being kicked back for corrections.

5. Adjudication & payment

The payer reviews the claim and decides what it will pay. Payment and a remittance advice (an 835 transaction) are posted to the account, and an Explanation of Benefits (EOB) spells out what was paid, adjusted, or denied.

6. Denials, appeals & billing

Denied claims are researched and appealed before filing deadlines pass. Any remaining balance is billed to the patient and collected — and then the cycle begins again for the next encounter.

Why it matters: a smooth cycle means healthy cash flow

Every stage protects revenue. Teams track metrics like these to catch problems early:

MetricWhat it measuresGood target
Clean claim rate% of claims accepted the first time95%+
Days in A/RAverage days to collect≤ 40
Denial rate% of claims denied< 5–10%

Clean claims get paid faster, denials cost time and money to resolve, and aged receivables get harder to collect the longer they sit — so accuracy early in the cycle saves real work later.

Coding sits at the center of it all

From registration to the final payment, every stage depends on accurate work — and skilled coders keep the whole cycle running smoothly. If you're studying for a coding certification, understanding this full cycle (not just the code sets) is exactly what interviewers are checking for.
