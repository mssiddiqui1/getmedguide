Introduction to CRC

What risk adjustment coding is, how HCCs and RAF scores work, and what the CRC credential tests.

What CRC is, and why risk adjustment exists

The Certified Risk Adjustment Coder (CRC), from AAPC, is a payer-side specialty focused on risk adjustment coding — the system that determines how much Medicare Advantage and ACA marketplace plans get paid based on how sick their enrolled population actually is.

The idea: a plan covering many patients with chronic, complex conditions should be paid more than one covering mostly healthy patients. Risk adjustment coding is what makes that adjustment possible — and it depends entirely on diagnoses being documented and coded accurately every single year.

The risk adjustment flow

Every step depends on the one before it — a missed diagnosis on the chart means it never reaches the RAF score at all.

Key concepts

ConceptWhat it means
HCCHierarchical Condition Category — groups of related, chronic ICD-10-CM diagnoses that carry payment weight
RAF scoreRisk Adjustment Factor — a numeric score built from a patient's HCCs (plus demographics) that predicts expected cost
MEAT criteriaMonitor, Evaluate, Assess/Address, Treat — the documentation standard a condition must meet each year to be codeable for risk adjustment
Annual recaptureChronic HCCs must be documented every calendar year — they don't carry forward automatically

Note: risk adjustment coding is a major compliance focus area for CMS and the OIG, precisely because overstated diagnoses inflate plan payments. CRC coders and auditors play a real role in catching both under- and over-coding.

Who should pursue CRC

Coders and auditors working for health plans, Medicare Advantage organizations, or provider groups that participate in value-based/risk contracts — a genuinely different work environment from a physician practice or hospital coding department.
